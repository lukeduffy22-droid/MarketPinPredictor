2. Structural problems and where to improve

Bluntly, the file is too thin and too narrow for the job you want it to do.

Concrete flaws:

Only SPX, no NDX or RUT

Nothing in this CSV identifies the index symbol or includes any NDX or RUT data.

You are trying to rank SPX vs NDX vs RUT for 0DTE plays using only SPX structural data.

Sampling is far too sparse

Nine rows for the entire morning, then nothing between 12:00 PM ET and your “T-35 min to close” decision point on a 1:00 PM ET early close day. 
The Economic Times

Pin behavior in the final 60–30 minutes is exactly where the edge you want either appears or disappears; you have zero direct data for that period in this export.

Total GEX vs Net GEX are identical

In the file, Total GEX and Net GEX are the same value at every timestamp.

That means one of two things:

You are not capturing gross vs net gamma separately and have effectively duplicated the same metric in two columns, or

The provider is only giving you net, and you labeled one column incorrectly.

You are not currently seeing how balanced the book is (gross) versus directional tilt (net).

No feature engineering in the export

You have the raw Distance and Pull Strength, but nothing like:

Normalized distance: distance in points divided by 1-day ATR or by average daily move.

Pin drift rate: change in pin per hour.

Gamma gradient or concentration at nearby strikes.

For model use, you want those engineered features baked into the dataset you feed your predictor.

Time window is biased toward the calmest part of the day

All rows are between 09:45–12:00 ET on a holiday half-session.

No open auction, no last-hour flows, no closing auction effect captured in this file.

Summary:

As “is this data garbage” → no.

As “is this enough to reliably time last-30-minute 0DTE bets” → no.

Replicate the exact same schema for NDX and RUT

Gamma structure visibility

Your uploaded file only gives structural info for SPX.

You know where your SPX gamma pin roughly is (6840) and that spot is locked to it within ±0.03% for the recorded window.

For NDX and RUT, you currently lack equivalent pin data in this export.

Liquidity and spreads

SPX and NDX 0DTE options typically have tighter spreads and deeper order books than RUT.

RUT offers greater percentage swings but worse execution quality and slippage in many strikes.

Given those facts:

If you are trading based on your gamma-pin framework from this CSV, SPX is the only index where you actually have structural information in front of you, and it currently shows a classic positive-gamma pinning environment around 6,840. That supports very small expected last-minutes moves rather than explosive ones.

If you are purely hunting for the largest potential last-30-minute percentage move, RUT has the highest realized intraday range today and historically tends to be more volatile, so it is the most convex but also the least controlled instrument for 0DTE lottery-style trades.

None of this turns into a deterministic “buy here and you make money” rule. With 35 minutes left on a half-day, the realistic expectation is that all three indices mostly churn near current levels; any bet on a 2–3 strike, last-minutes melt in either direction is high-variance and dominated by noise, not by the gamma snapshot alone.
