Key point: that does not mean the gamma snapshot engine is “better” in general; it means that 2 minutes before the close on a pinned, high-gamma, half-day session, there simply is not much left to predict. A naive “close = last price” baseline is already very strong at that horizon. Your gamma snapshot mostly confirms “no explosive move coming,” which is what happened.

Where the big model failed and how to fix it:

1. **Systematic downward bias on an up-close day**

   * All three indices finished **above** your EOM predictions.
   * That indicates the model either:

     * Underestimates late-session drift on strong days, or
     * Is not aware that this is a month-end, half-day pattern where dealers may allow a small markup into the close rather than a mean-revert.

   Action:

   * Refit or recalibrate the intercept and drift terms using recent data.
   * Explicitly compute and store residuals (actual − predicted) over the last N sessions and subtract the rolling mean residual from future forecasts.

2. **Wrong regime handling for half-days and special sessions**

   * Today is Black Friday, early close, thinner liquidity, different intraday volume curve.
   * If your EOM model was trained mostly on **full** sessions, it is implicitly assuming 6.5 hours of intraday dynamics, not 3.5.
   * Result: the model thinks the last 5–30 minutes behave like a normal day’s last 5–30, which they do not.

   Action:

   * Tag every training day with regime flags:

     * `is_half_day`, `is_holiday_adjacent`, `is_EOM`, `is_EOW`, `VIX_regime`, etc.
   * Either train a separate model for half-days and EOM days or include those flags as features and let the model learn different behavior.

3. **Horizon mismatch**

   * Your EOM model is a “large” end-of-day predictor, likely built from data much earlier in the session and longer-horizon features.
   * By the time you are 5–30 minutes from the close, the binding constraints become:

     * Current spot
     * Gamma structure
     * Short-term microtrend
   * The big model is not designed as a 5-minute-to-close forecaster; it is a session-level forecaster being used outside its ideal horizon.

   Action:

   * Train a separate **short-horizon close model** using only data from, say, T−60 to T−5 minutes with high-frequency features:

     * Last 5–10 minute return
     * Distance to VWAP
     * Change in gamma pin over last 30 minutes
   * Use the big EOM model earlier in the day and fade it out in favor of the short-horizon model plus gamma snapshot as you approach the close.

4. **No explicit use of the gamma snapshot inside the EOM forecast**

   * Right now, gamma snapshot and EOM model look like two independent systems.
   * The gamma snapshot near the close is strong evidence of where the book wants to settle.
   * The EOM model is ignoring that evidence, which is why, 5 minutes from the bell, it can still be 0.5% off.

   Action:

   * Add features to the short-horizon model such as:

     * `distance_spot_to_pin`
     * `pin_drift_last_30min`
     * `net_gamma_level` and maybe log scale
   * Penalize forecasts that diverge heavily from “spot ≈ pin” in a high-positive-gamma regime.

5. **Lack of explicit error modelling**

   * You are looking only at point forecasts (single number) and eyeballing distance.
   * The real edge is in **forecast distribution**, not a single point.

   Action:

   * For each horizon and regime, estimate not just mean prediction but also typical absolute error:

     * `MAE_full_days`, `MAE_half_days`, `MAE_EOM`.
   * Translate that into bands: “SPX close ∈ prediction ± X pts with 68% probability”.
   * Use those bands to decide if there is enough expected move vs premium to justify a 0DTE trade.

Interpretation:

* The **gamma snapshot** did its job: it told you “we are pinned around 6,840; do not expect a huge move” and the close respected that.
* The **big EOM model** produced a low-biased estimate that ignored:

  * Regime (half-day, month-end),
  * High gamma pinning,
  * Immediate microtrend in the final minutes.

Repurpose the big model for what it is good at (early- to mid-session directional anchor and path expectation) and build a dedicated, regime-aware, short-horizon close model that explicitly ingests the gamma snapshot and microstructure when you are inside the last hour.
